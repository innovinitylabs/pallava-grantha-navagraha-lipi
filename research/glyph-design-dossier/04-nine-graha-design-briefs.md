# Nava Graha Lipi — Glyph Design Research Dossier
## Document 04: Nine Graha Individual Creative & Technical Design Briefs

---

### Introduction & Methodological Rules for Fable Designers

This document provides exhaustive, individual creative design briefs for each of the Nine Grahas (*Navagraha*).

For every Graha, designers in Fable must explore **three distinct, original visual directions**:
1. **Direction A — Conservative Pallava Ductus:** Rooted in the structural stroke ductus, chisel serifs, and loop geometry of 5th–8th century CE Pallava Grantha epigraphy.
2. **Direction B — Geometric Yantra Synthesis:** Synthesizing the traditional geometric archetype (*maṇḍala/yantra*) with modern typographic line weights and optical compensations.
3. **Direction C — Modern Calligraphic Flourish:** An open, fluid silhouette inspired by Vatteluttu and Tamil-Brahmi cursive acceleration, optimized for contemporary high-DPI screens and small-scale digital panchangam charts.

> [!CAUTION]
> **No Final Winners Declared:** These briefs define the conceptual territory, structural constraints, and open questions. Designers must author concepts across all three directions so that the research collective can evaluate them comparatively.

### 1. SŪRYA (சூரியன்) — The Sun

#### 1.1 Identity & Transliteration
- **Canonical Latin (IAST):** Sūrya  
- **Common English:** Surya / Sol / The Sun  
- **Modern Tamil:** சூரியன் (Cūriyaṉ) / ஞாயிறு (Ñāyiṟu)  
- **Epigraphic Variants:** Pallava Grantha solar crown dedications; Tamil-Brahmi solar bindu notations.  
- **Target PUA Allocation:** `U+E000`

#### 1.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role
- **Status:** `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`  
- **Role:** Sovereign luminary (*Rāja-graha*), origin of the planetary week, source of cosmic illumination, life force (*Prāṇa*), soul (*Ātman*).
- **Element:** Fire (*Agni*).  
- **Direction:** East (*Kizhakku*).  
- **Weekday:** Sunday (Tamil: *Ñāyiṟṟukkiḻamai* / ஞாயிற்றுக்கிழமை).  
- **Traditional Yantra Geometry:** Circle (*Vṛtta*), circular eight-petaled lotus (*Aṣṭadala Padma*).  
- **Iconographic Attributes in Tamil Temples:** Two lotuses (*Padma*) held aloft in both hands; seven-horse chariot (*Saptāśva ratha*); solar disk halo (*Prabhāmaṇḍala*); cylindrical crown (*Karaṇḍa Makuṭa*).

#### 1.3 Relevant Historical Evidence
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Standing Sūrya relief carvings at the 7th-century Dharmaraja Ratha (Mahabalipuram) and the 8th-century Kailasanatha Temple (Kanchipuram), exhibiting circular nimbuses behind cylindrical crowns.
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Solar symbols on early Chera, Chola, and Pandya punch-marked and cast copper coins featuring a central orb surrounded by four or eight rays/dots.
- `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`: Early Indian solar worship emphasized the unbroken circle with central focus (*Bindu*) as the symbol of undivided unity (*Ekatvam*).

#### 1.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors
- The solar orb (*Maṇḍala*).
- The central seed-point (*Bindu*) radiating outward into space.
- Eight cardinal and ordinal rays (*Aṣṭa-dik-pāla* radiance).
- The twin lotuses of the morning and evening sky.

#### 1.5 Relevant Tamil-Region Design Influences
- Pallava stone carving: Royal parasol (*Chattra*) and solar crown curves.
- Tamil-Brahmi: The archaic isolated dot (*Puḷḷi* / *Bindu*), rendered with absolute minimalist purity.

#### 1.6 Three Original Design Directions for Fable
```
  DIRECTION A (Pallava Ductus)      DIRECTION B (Yantra Synthesis)      DIRECTION C (Calligraphic)
        ╭───────╮                             ◇                              ╭─────╮
       │    •    │                         ╱  │  ╲                         ╭─╯     │
       │  (Bindu)│                        │ ╭─┴─╮ │                        │   •   │
        ╰───────╯                         │ │ • │ │                        ╰───────╯
       Chisel Serifs                      ╰─┬─╯                            Spiral Coil
      at Cardinal Axes                   Rhombic Grid                     Vatteluttu Sweep
```

1. **Direction A (Conservative Pallava Ductus):**
   - *Concept:* A robust circular solar orb resting on a grounded base, featuring subtle Pallava horizontal chisel serifs at the cardinal apexes (North, South, East, West) and an unchoked central bindu.
   - *Strengths:* Dignified, architectural, unmistakably grounded in South Indian epigraphy.
   - *Weaknesses:* Must avoid looking like a ship's helm or Western cross-in-circle (Earth symbol ♁).
2. **Direction B (Geometric Yantra Synthesis):**
   - *Concept:* A concentric synthesis of a rhombic diamond outer boundary enclosing an inner circular orb with a solid central bindu.
   - *Strengths:* Exceptional visual energy; stands out sharply from round letterforms in text.
   - *Weaknesses:* Higher vector complexity; risk of stroke merging at 12pt text size.
3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* An unbroken, single-stroke spiral ductus that initiates at the outer perimeter and curves fluidly inward into the central bindu, inspired by Vatteluttu curvilinear economy.
   - *Strengths:* Highly dynamic, contemporary, suggests rotational solar motion.
   - *Weaknesses:* May look more like a cursive numeral or letter than an astronomical glyph.

#### 1.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Risk:* Calling any of these designs "the ancient Pallava Sun glyph." (Pallava Grantha had no single Sun glyph; it spelled *Sū-rya* phonetically).
- *Western Conflation Risk:* Avoid duplicating the Western astrological Sun symbol (☉, simple circle with center dot, `U+2609`). The Indian glyph must incorporate distinctive Tamil-region ductus, cardinal terminals, or petal nuances.

#### 1.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- Should the central bindu be connected to the outer ring via rays, or should it float independently in open counter space?
- How prominent should the cardinal ray flares be to avoid visual noise at 12pt?

### 2. CANDRA / CHANDRA (சந்திரன்) — The Moon

#### 2.1 Identity & Transliteration
- **Canonical Latin (IAST):** Candra / Chandra  
- **Common English:** Chandra / Luna / The Moon  
- **Modern Tamil:** சந்திரன் (Cantiraṉ) / திங்கள் (Tiṅkaḷ)  
- **Epigraphic Variants:** Pallava lunar crest carvings; Vatteluttu curved bowl ligatures.  
- **Target PUA Allocation:** `U+E001`

#### 2.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role
- **Status:** `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`  
- **Role:** Night luminary, sovereign of the mind (*Manas*), presiding deity of waters, tides, herbs, and soma nectar.
- **Element:** Water (*Ap* / *Jala*).  
- **Direction:** North-East (*Vada-Kizhakku*).  
- **Weekday:** Monday (Tamil: *Tiṅkaḷkiḻamai* / திங்கள்கிழமை).  
- **Traditional Yantra Geometry:** Semicircle, crescent (*Ardhacandra*).  
- **Iconographic Attributes in Tamil Temples:** Ten-horse chariot; holding white water lilies (*Kumuda*); crescent hair adornment (*Candra-kalā*).

#### 2.3 Relevant Historical Evidence
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Crescent moon motifs sculpted on Śiva's crown (*Candraśekhara*) across 7th–9th century Pallava rock steles at Kanchipuram and Mahabalipuram.
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Horizontal crescent bowls serving as bases in early South Indian coinage and copper-plate sign-manual emblems.
- `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`: In Indian tradition, the crescent is frequently depicted horizontally—as an upward-facing open cup or boat (*Naukā*) that holds the celestial soma nectar—rather than the vertically standing Western crescent.

#### 2.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors
- The upward-opening lunar crescent basin (*Soma-pātra* / celestial cup).
- The supportive horizontal pedestal grounding celestial rhythms.
- The waxing phase (*Śukla-pakṣa*) curved arc.

#### 2.5 Relevant Tamil-Region Design Influences
- Pallava stone carving: The broad, deep sweep of the royal crescent crown.
- Grantha script: The smooth, rounded lower bowl of Grantha letters *MA* and *YA*.

#### 2.6 Three Original Design Directions for Fable
1. **Direction A (Conservative Pallava Ductus):**
   - *Concept:* A deep, upward-opening horizontal crescent bowl resting on the baseline, featuring weighted Pallava serif terminals at both horn tips.
   - *Strengths:* Unmistakably Indian; directly avoids the vertical Western crescent (☽, `U+263D`).
   - *Weaknesses:* Must maintain enough vertical presence so as not to appear merely like an underline or diacritic.
2. **Direction B (Geometric Yantra Synthesis):**
   - *Concept:* A composite crescent intersecting an inner subtle secondary circle or droplet representing the nectar of Soma.
   - *Strengths:* Rich symbolic resonance; balanced mass distribution across the 1000 UPM grid.
   - *Weaknesses:* Potential visual clutter at text sizes below 14pt.
3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* An asymmetrical sweeping crescent with a thick parabolic lower belly that thins elegantly into a weighted terminal horn.
   - *Strengths:* Exceptional lyricism and grace; fluid reading rhythm.
   - *Weaknesses:* Requires careful optical tuning to prevent one side from appearing too heavy.

#### 2.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Western Conflation Risk:* If drawn as a vertical crescent facing left or right (☽ / ☾), it is indistinguishable from Western astrology. The horizontal or tilted upward-receptive orientation is essential for Indian cultural integrity.

#### 2.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- Should the lunar horns terminate at equal heights along Cap-Height, or should an intentional asymmetrical tilt convey movement?
- Should the interior space of the crescent hold a central bindu/droplet, or remain completely open?

### 3. MAṄGALA (செவ்வாய்) — Mars

#### 3.1 Identity & Transliteration
- **Canonical Latin (IAST):** Maṅgala  
- **Common English:** Mangala / Mars / The Red Planet  
- **Modern Tamil:** செவ்வாய் (Cevvāy, lit. "The Red One")  
- **Epigraphic Variants:** Pallava spearhead (*Vēl*) dedications; Tamil-Brahmi angular apex steles.  
- **Target PUA Allocation:** `U+E002`

#### 3.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role
- **Status:** `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`  
- **Role:** Planetary commander (*Senāpati*), deity of courage, land (*Bhūmi-putra*), energy, valor, and fire.
- **Element:** Fire (*Agni*).  
- **Direction:** South (*Therku*).  
- **Weekday:** Tuesday (Tamil: *Cevvāykkiḻamai* / செவ்வாய்க்கிழமை).  
- **Traditional Yantra Geometry:** Upward-pointing equilateral triangle (*Trikoṇa*).  
- **Iconographic Attributes in Tamil Temples:** Mount is a ram (*Meṣa*); holding the divine spear/lance (*Śakti* / Tamil *Vēl*); deep vermilion red complexion.

#### 3.3 Relevant Historical Evidence
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: The *Vēl* (divine spear with broad leaf blade and pointed tip) is one of the oldest epigraphically and archaeologically attested symbols in the Tamil region, found on Iron Age megalithic burials (Adichanallur) and early Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions.
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Pallava-era relief carvings depict Maṅgala holding a lance with a triangular leaf head.
- `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`: The upward-pointing triangle (*Trikoṇa*) universally represents the ascending fire element (*Agni*) in Indian geometric treatises (*Śilparatna*).

#### 3.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors
- The acute ascending spear-head (*Vēl* blade).
- The upward-pointing fire triangle (*Agni-trikoṇa*).
- Dynamic angular ascension and martial energy.

#### 3.5 Relevant Tamil-Region Design Influences
- Megalithic Tamil *Vēl* contours: Leaf-shaped blade with a rounded central rib and sharp apex.
- Tamil-Brahmi letter *E* (archaic triangle form).

#### 3.6 Three Original Design Directions for Fable
1. **Direction A (Conservative Pallava Ductus):**
   - *Concept:* An acute triangular spear blade mounted on a short vertical stem, finished with Pallava chisel horizontal notches at the lower barb tips.
   - *Strengths:* Imposing martial dignity; profoundly rooted in Tamil epigraphical iconography.
   - *Weaknesses:* Needs care so it does not look like an ordinary arrow cursor.
2. **Direction B (Geometric Yantra Synthesis):**
   - *Concept:* A pure equilateral or acute triangle enclosing a central vertical energy spine, resting on a stable horizontal baseline bar.
   - *Strengths:* Powerful, minimalist, easily recognizable at 11pt size.
   - *Weaknesses:* Could look overly generic if not enriched by subtle optical curvature along the sides.
3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* A dynamic two-stroke assembly: an ascending diagonal stroke snapping sharply into an acute descending spear apex, with an open Barb.
   - *Strengths:* Kinetic, aggressive, highly modern.
   - *Weaknesses:* Asymmetry may disrupt horizontal text flow if uncounterbalanced.

#### 3.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Western Conflation Risk:* Must NOT replicate the circle with diagonal upper-right arrow (♂, shield and spear of Mars, `U+2642`). The Indian Maṅgala glyph must center on the upward fire-triangle or symmetrical Tamil *Vēl* silhouette.

#### 3.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- Should the triangular blade have straight edges (geometric) or slightly convex leaf-curved edges (calligraphic *Vēl*)?
- Does it require a grounding baseline stem, or should the triangle float freely?

#### 4.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role

- **Direction:** North (*Vadakku*).

- **Traditional Yantra Geometry:** Curved bow (*Dhanuṣ*) or horizontal rectangle.  
- **Iconographic Attributes in Tamil Temples:** Mount is a lion or winged creature; holding a bow and arrow or manuscript; green complexion.

#### 4.3 Relevant Historical Evidence

- `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`: In Siddhāntic astronomy, Budha's rapid epicyclic motion and close solar proximity made it a symbol of swiftness, duality, and intellectual adaptability.

#### 4.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors

3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* A dual-winged looping contour forming an open caduceus-like trajectory that curls upward with feather-light terminals.
   - *Strengths:* Conveys intelligence, speed, and lightness.
   - *Weaknesses:* Delicate loops may close up at small screen resolutions.

#### 4.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Western Conflation Risk:* Do not copy the Western Mercury symbol (☿, `U+263F`, cross beneath circle with horned cap). The Indian glyph must derive from the bow (*Dhanuṣ*) or bi-lobed intellect geometry.

#### 4.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- Should the bow be oriented vertically (standing) or horizontally (spanning)?
- How to ensure the loops remain open at 12pt?

### 5. GURU / BṚHASPATI (குரு / வியாழன்) — Jupiter

- **Target PUA Allocation:** `U+E004`

#### 5.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role
- **Status:** `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`

- **Element:** Ether / Space (*Ākāśa*).  
- **Direction:** North (*Vadakku*).  
- **Weekday:** Thursday (Tamil: *Viyāḻakkiḻamai* / வியாழக்கிழமை).  
- **Traditional Yantra Geometry:** Rectangle (*Dīrgha-caturaśra*), square pedestal (*Pīṭha*).  
- **Iconographic Attributes in Tamil Temples:** Mount is an elephant (*Airāvata*); holding palm-leaf scriptures (*Tālapātra* / *Grantha*) and prayer beads (*Akṣamālā*); golden-yellow complexion.

#### 5.3 Relevant Historical Evidence
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Pallava monumental temple pillars (e.g., Mamallapuram caves) feature massive rectangular bases and segmented shafts (*Stambha*), symbolizing cosmic stability and spiritual authority.
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Guru is universally placed in the southern or northern honored niche in South Indian temple navagraha shrines.

#### 5.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors
- The monumental pillar (*Stambha*) upholding cosmic law.
- The rectangular teaching throne or scriptural pedestal (*Pīṭha*).
- The four-square enclosure of expansive wisdom.

#### 5.5 Relevant Tamil-Region Design Influences
- Pallava rock architecture: The monumental square pilasters and sanctum door jambs.
- Grantha character *GA*: The solemn, grounded arch with architectural stability.

#### 5.6 Three Original Design Directions for Fable
1. **Direction A (Conservative Pallava Ductus):**
   - *Concept:* A stately monumental gate/pillar structure composed of two vertical stems capped by a heavy Pallava horizontal lintel with downward chisel spurs.
   - *Strengths:* Immense architectural presence; feels ancient, sacred, and authoritative.
   - *Weaknesses:* Must avoid looking like a Greek Pi (Π) or Japanese Torii gate.
2. **Direction B (Geometric Yantra Synthesis):**
   - *Concept:* A golden-section rectangle oriented vertically or horizontally, enclosing an inner sanctum bindu or lotus crossbar.
   - *Strengths:* Pure geometry; instantly distinct from circular and triangular planetary symbols.
   - *Weaknesses:* Needs distinctive epigraphic terminals so it does not look like an unassigned Unicode missing-glyph box.
3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* An expansive open arch resting on weighted base pedestals, with a subtle vertical spire emerging from the top center.
   - *Strengths:* Combines monumental dignity with soaring spiritual aspiration.
   - *Weaknesses:* Vertical spire must not overshoot the Ascender line (`Y=150`).

#### 5.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Western Conflation Risk:* Avoid the Western Jupiter glyph (♃, `U+2643`, stylized numeral 4 / zeta). The Indian Guru glyph must convey architectural immovability and Dharmic stability.

#### 5.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- How to ensure the rectangular enclosure does not resemble an empty glyph box ("tofu") in fonts?
- What internal elements will best communicate the teaching authority of the Guru?

#### 6.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role

#### 6.3 Relevant Historical Evidence
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Pallava decorative friezes frequently incorporate five-fold floral rosettes and continuous interwoven knots (*Brahma-mudi*).
- `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`: Astronomical cycles of Venus trace a near-perfect pentagram in the sky over an 8-year synodic cycle, which aligns with traditional Indian pentagonal yantra geometry.

#### 6.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors
- The five-fold symmetry of the Venusian orbital rose.
- The continuous interwoven knot (*Knot of Beauty*).
- Symmetrical blooming floral petals (*Padma-dala*).

#### 6.5 Relevant Tamil-Region Design Influences

#### 6.6 Three Original Design Directions for Fable

   - *Strengths:* Elegant, organic, unmistakably South Indian calligraphic art.
   - *Weaknesses:* Complexity requires strict counter-space calibration to prevent fill-in.
2. **Direction B (Geometric Yantra Synthesis):**
   - *Concept:* A stylized pentagonal framework where five apexes connect through continuous harmonious curved paths.
   - *Strengths:* Direct astronomical alignment with the 8-year Venus synodic cycle; geometric perfection.
   - *Weaknesses:* Can become difficult to draw with clean cubic Béziers if overlapping paths are not resolved into a single contour.
3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* An unbroken, single-line figure-eight or infinity-loop expanding into an upper blossoming petal, grounded on an elegant horizontal base.
   - *Strengths:* Fluid, graceful, stunning on digital displays.
   - *Weaknesses:* Must maintain enough unique visual cues so as not to be confused with a mathematical infinity sign (∞).

#### 6.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Western Conflation Risk:* Do NOT duplicate the Western Venus mirror (♀, `U+2640`). The Indian Śukra symbol must embody floral harmony, pentagonal geometry, or continuous woven knotwork.

#### 6.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- Should the glyph emphasize floral petals or interwoven knot loops?
- How to keep the negative spaces open when the glyph is reduced to 12pt?

### 8. RĀHU (இராகு) — The Ascending Lunar Node

#### 8.1 Identity & Transliteration
- **Canonical Latin (IAST):** Rāhu  
- **Common English:** Rahu / The Ascending Node / Dragon's Head  
- **Modern Tamil:** இராகு (Irāku) / ராகு (Rāku)  
- **Epigraphic Variants:** Pallava serpentine hood motifs; Vatteluttu ascending coiling arcs.  
- **Target PUA Allocation:** `U+E007`

#### 8.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role
- **Status:** `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`  
- **Role:** Shadow planet (*Chāyā-graha*), ascending intersection point of the lunar orbit and the ecliptic, eclipse causer (*Svarbhānu*), deity of cosmic ambition, obsession, and occult shadow.
- **Element:** Shadow / Cosmic darkness (*Tamas*).  
- **Direction:** South-West (*Then-Merkku*).  
- **Weekday:** No dedicated weekday; rules *Rāhu-kālam* daily.  
- **Traditional Yantra Geometry:** Winnowing fan / Serpent hood (*Śūrpa* / *Phaṇa*), crescent shadow.  
- **Iconographic Attributes in Tamil Temples:** Colossal severed head or bust with wide fangs and a five-headed cobra canopy (*Nāga-chattra*).

#### 8.3 Relevant Historical Evidence
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Pallava relief panels (e.g., Mahabalipuram Mahishasuramardini cave) depict serpent canopies with flared hood geometries.
- `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`: In Siddhāntic astronomy, Rāhu is not a physical rock or gas giant, but an exact mathematical intersection point where the Moon crosses the ecliptic from south to north, causing solar and lunar eclipses when orbital alignments coincide.

#### 8.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors
- The expanding serpentine cobra hood (*Nāga-phaṇa*).
- The upward-coiling ascending orbital vortex.
- The shadow arc obscuring the solar disc during an eclipse.

#### 8.5 Relevant Tamil-Region Design Influences
- Pallava *Nāga* carvings: Smooth, muscular curvature expanding into a broad rounded hood.
- Vatteluttu script: Fluid, looping, continuous spiral energy.

#### 8.6 Three Original Design Directions for Fable
1. **Direction A (Conservative Pallava Ductus):**
   - *Concept:* An ascending vertical stem that broadens outward into a dignified, tripartite flared serpent hood at Cap-Height, terminating in classical Pallava chisel notches.
   - *Strengths:* Instantly recognizable as the eclipse serpent canopy; magnificent cultural presence.
   - *Weaknesses:* Must avoid becoming too literal an illustration of a snake.
2. **Direction B (Geometric Yantra Synthesis):**
   - *Concept:* An upward-expanding parabolic arc enclosing a dark eclipse shadow crescent, supported by an ascending diagonal axis.
   - *Strengths:* Pure astronomical metaphor; communicates orbital node crossing without mythological caricature.
   - *Weaknesses:* Demands careful optical tuning to prevent looking like an upside-down umbrella.
3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* A single continuous counter-clockwise spiral that rises from the baseline, coils tightly outward into an upper loop, and flares into a wide protective hood.
   - *Strengths:* Brilliant kinetic motion; looks marvelous in animation (Fable).
   - *Weaknesses:* Inner spiral coil must not fill in at 12pt text size.

#### 8.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Western Conflation Risk:* Do not copy the Caput Draconis loop (☊, `U+260A`). The Indian Rāhu symbol must express the authentic Tamil *Nāga-phaṇa* (serpent hood) or ascending eclipse vortex.

#### 8.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- How abstract should the serpent hood be? (Aim for a typographic glyph, not a detailed illustration).
- Should the ascending nodal motion point toward the top-right or remain vertically centered?

### 9. KETU (கேது) — The Descending Lunar Node

#### 9.1 Identity & Transliteration
- **Canonical Latin (IAST):** Ketu  
- **Common English:** Ketu / The Descending Node / Dragon's Tail / The Comet  
- **Modern Tamil:** கேது (Kētu)  
- **Epigraphic Variants:** Pallava temple banner carvings; Vatteluttu descending terminal hooks.  
- **Target PUA Allocation:** `U+E008`

#### 9.2 Traditional Associations & Cosmological Role
- **Status:** `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`  
- **Role:** Shadow planet (*Chāyā-graha*), descending intersection point of the lunar orbit and the ecliptic, deity of spiritual liberation (*Mokṣa-kāraka*), ascetic detachment, comets, and cosmic dissolution.
- **Element:** Shadow / Spiritual fire (*Mokṣa-agni*).  
- **Direction:** North-West (*Vada-Merkku*).  
- **Weekday:** No dedicated weekday; rules complementary shadowy planetary periods.  
- **Traditional Yantra Geometry:** Inverted flag (*Dhvaja* / *Patāka*), comet tail (*Puccha*).  
- **Iconographic Attributes in Tamil Temples:** Human upper torso with hands folded in anjali mudra, terminating in a coiled serpent tail or comet plume.

#### 9.3 Relevant Historical Evidence
- `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`: Pallava temple steles depict temple flagstaffs (*Dhvajastambha*) with fluttering triangular victory banners.
- `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`: Ketu in Sanskrit literally denotes a comet, meteor, or prominent banner in the sky. In Siddhāntic astronomy, it represents the descending orbital crossing from north to south.

#### 9.4 Symbolic Possibilities & Visual Metaphors
- The temple flag (*Dhvaja*) fluttering in the wind.
- The descending comet tail (*Puccha*) tapering downward into space.
- The inverted complementary twin of Rāhu.

#### 9.5 Relevant Tamil-Region Design Influences
- Pallava stone carving: The dynamic, waving folds of temple festival banners.
- Grantha character *LA* and *RA*: Graceful descending terminal loops.

#### 9.6 Three Original Design Directions for Fable
1. **Direction A (Conservative Pallava Ductus):**
   - *Concept:* A vertical staff extending downward from Cap-Height, supporting an angular triangular or scalloped Pallava banner flag that flutters outward, grounded by a terminal hook.
   - *Strengths:* Highly distinctive; direct textual fidelity to the *Dhvaja* iconographic archetype.
   - *Weaknesses:* Must avoid confusion with golf flag icons or modern interface bookmark symbols.
2. **Direction B (Geometric Yantra Synthesis):**
   - *Concept:* A downward-tapering chevron or inverted funnel intersecting an orbital axis line, indicating descending nodal motion.
   - *Strengths:* Mathematically pure complementary pair to the ascending Rāhu node.
   - *Weaknesses:* Needs distinctive epigraphic weight to avoid looking like a downward arrow.
3. **Direction C (Modern Calligraphic Flourish):**
   - *Concept:* An inverted calligraphic flourish that starts with a dignified upper knot and trails downward into a sweeping, tapering comet plume.
   - *Strengths:* Evocative of spiritual detachment (*Mokṣa*); beautiful flowing line quality.
   - *Weaknesses:* Descending tail must not drop so low that it clashes with descenders of adjacent letters.

#### 9.7 Potential Misleading Historical Representations & Risks
- *Western Conflation Risk:* Do not copy Cauda Draconis (☋, `U+260B`). The Indian Ketu glyph must convey the flying temple banner (*Dhvaja*) or the descending comet tail.

#### 9.8 Critical Design Questions for Human Judgement
- Should Ketu visually mirror Rāhu in symmetry, or should it stand as a completely independent design archetype (flag vs. serpent hood)?
- Which direction should the banner flag flutter (left or right)? Standard typographic convention favors pointing rightward with reading flow.
