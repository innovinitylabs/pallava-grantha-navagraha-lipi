### 1. Introduction: Provenance Standards & Epistemic Honesty

Every historical citation, inscriptional reference, and palaeographical deduction utilized in the Nava Graha Lipi initiative must be verifiable through legitimate scholarly publications, published archaeological reports, or primary inscriptional estampages.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Strict Anti-Fabrication Rule:**
> If a particular historical form, planetary notation, or inscriptional ligature is not attested in published academic corpora, this dossier explicitly marks it as **"UNATTESTED / HISTORICAL GAP."** 
> 
> Under no circumstances do we invent fictional inscriptions, fabricate copper-plate citations, or present 20th-century astrological line drawings as ancient archaeological discoveries.

#### 2.2 Unicode Consortium Character Encoding Proposals
- **Author:** Anshuman Pandey.
- **Title:** *Preliminary Proposal to Encode Pallava in Unicode* (Document L2/18-083 / 18083-pallava.pdf).
- **Publication / Year:** Unicode Consortium Document Registry, March 26, 2018.
- **Repository Location:** `https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2018/18083-pallava.pdf` (local copy preserved at project root: `18083-pallava.pdf`).
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]` & `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`
  - Establishes a systematic proposed character inventory for Pallava script:
    - 9 independent vowels (A, AA, I, II, U, UU, E, AI, O).
    - 35 consonant letters (Velar, Palatal, Retroflex, Dental, Labial, Sonorants, Sibilants, Gutturals).
    - 9 dependent vowel signs, 2 length marks, Virama, Subjoiner.
    - Punctuation (Pallava Danda, Double Danda, Siddham sign).
  - Includes comparative script tables comparing Ashokan Brahmi, Southern Brahmi, Early Pallava, Middle Pallava, and Later Grantha.
  - Serves as the primary reference dataset for the 61 catalogued characters in Nava Graha Lipi's Script Atlas.
- **Source Reliability:** High scholarly authority; rigorous Unicode encoding analysis.
- **Copyright / Usage:** Open access technical document submitted to the Unicode Technical Committee (UTC).

- **Author / Institution:** Unicode Consortium.
- **Title:** *The Unicode Standard — Grantha Block (Range U+11300–U+1137F)*.
- **Publication / Year:** Unicode Consortium, Standard Version 7.0 (2014) to present.
- **Repository Location:** `https://www.unicode.org/charts/PDF/U11300.pdf`.
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`

- **Source Reliability:** Authoritative international character encoding standard.
- **Copyright / Usage:** Open standard (Unicode Consortium).

#### 2.3 Foundational Palaeographic Treatises
- **Author:** Arthur Coke Burnell.
- **Title:** *Elements of South Indian Palaeography, from the Fourth to the Seventeenth Century A.D.* (Second Enlarged Edition).
- **Publication / Year:** London: Trübner & Co., 1878.
- **Repository Location:** British Library; Government Museum Chennai; archive.org.
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`
  - First comprehensive comparative study of South Indian palaeography: distinguishes the Eastern Chalukya, Pallava, Chera, and Chola script lineages.
  - Documents the palaeographic evolution from early Vatteluttu ("round script") and Pallava Grantha into medieval South Indian scripts.
  - Plate II–IV provide exact hand-drawn facsimiles of Pallava inscriptions showing chisel stroke ductus and terminal flares.
- **Source Reliability:** High foundational historical value; essential for understanding the transition from chisel to palm-leaf writing.
- **Copyright / Usage:** Public domain.

- **Author:** Georg Bühler.
- **Title:** *Indische Palaeographie von circa 350 a. Chr. bis circa 1300 n. Chr.*
- **Publication / Year:** Strassburg: Karl J. Trübner, 1896. (English translation: *Indian Palaeography*, edited by J.F. Fleet, Appendix to *Indian Antiquary*, 1904).
- **Repository Location:** Major university research libraries; archive.org.
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`
  - Foundational classification of the Southern group of Indian alphabets (Section 7, "The Southern Alphabets").
  - Identifies the distinguishing characteristics of the Western and Central types vs. the Grantha-Pallava branch.
- **Source Reliability:** Classical standard reference in Indian palaeography.
- **Copyright / Usage:** Public domain.

- **Author:** Calambur Sivaramamurti.
- **Title:** *Indian Epigraphy and South Indian Scripts*.
- **Publication / Year:** Madras: Bulletin of the Madras Government Museum (New Series, General Section, Vol. III, No. 4), 1952.
- **Repository Location:** Government Museum, Chennai.
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]` & `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`
  - In-depth physical analysis of stone and bronze inscriptions in the Tamil region.
  - Contains extensive chronological comparative charts of South Indian script evolution from Ashokan Brahmi, Tamil-Brahmi, and Pallava, to Chola and Vijayanagara scripts.
  - Identifies the specific decorative tendencies of Pallava royal script carvers under Mahendravarman I (*Vichitrachitta*).
- **Source Reliability:** Outstanding epigraphical authority from a leading Indian archaeologist and museum curator.
- **Copyright / Usage:** Published museum bulletin; educational research usage.

- **Author:** Iravatham Mahadevan.
- **Title:** *Early Tamil Epigraphy: From the Earliest Times to the Sixth Century A.D.*
- **Publication / Year:** Chennai / Cambridge: Cre-A / Harvard University Department of Sanskrit and Indian Studies (Harvard Oriental Series, Vol. 62), 2003.
- **Repository Location:** Major academic libraries worldwide.
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]` & `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`
  - Definitive decipherment, corpus, and palaeographical study of Tamil-Brahmi rock shelter inscriptions (Mangulam, Pugalur, Arittapatti).
  - Documents the archaic *Puḷḷi* (virama dot) notation and early South Indian geometric letterforms.
  - Confirms the structural antiquity of the Tamil writing tradition independent of North Indian Devanagari lines.
- **Source Reliability:** The definitive benchmark work on early Tamil epigraphy.
- **Copyright / Usage:** Copyright Cre-A / Harvard University; cited for academic research.

### 3. Indian Astronomical & Jyotiṣa Literature

- **Author:** David Pingree.
- **Title:** *Jyotiḥśāstra: Astral and Mathematical Literature* (A History of Indian Literature, Vol. VI, Fasc. 4).
- **Publication / Year:** Wiesbaden: Otto Harrassowitz, 1981.
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION]`
  - Authoritative survey of the manuscript traditions of Indian astronomy and astrology.
  - Documents how planets were represented in mathematical tables (abbreviations and alphanumeric Katapayādi verse systems rather than universal abstract symbols).
- **Source Reliability:** Tier I academic history of science.

- **Author:** B. V. Subbarayappa & K. V. Sarma (Eds.).
- **Title:** *Indian Astronomy: A Source-Book*.
- **Publication / Year:** Bombay: Nehru Centre, 1985.
- **Relevant Research Claim:** `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]` & `[HISTORICALLY ATTESTED]`
  - Compiles translated excerpts from *Sūryasiddhānta*, *Āryabhaṭīya*, *Pañcasiddhāntikā*, and Tamil astronomical works regarding planetary parameters, nodal mechanics of Rāhu/Ketu, and planetary visual colors.
- **Source Reliability:** Authoritative scholarly compilation of primary textual sources.

- **Classical Text:** *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* of Varāhamihira (c. 6th century CE).
  - Relevant chapters: Planetary movements (*Graha-cāra*), planetary conflicts (*Graha-yuddha*), and iconographic descriptions.
  - Research Status: `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`. Documents the mythological attributes and directional alignments of the Navagraha.

- **Classical Text:** *Śilparatna* of Śrīkumāra (c. 16th century CE, Kerala / South India).
  - Relevant chapters: *Pratimā-lakṣaṇa* (sculptural proportions) and *Navagraha-yantra-lakṣaṇa*.
  - Research Status: `[TRADITIONAL ASSOCIATION]`. Provides textual confirmation for traditional geometric enclosures (circle for Sun, crescent for Moon, triangle for Mars, bow for Mercury, rectangle for Jupiter, pentagon for Venus, flag for Ketu).
