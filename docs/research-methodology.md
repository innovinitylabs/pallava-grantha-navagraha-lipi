# Nava Graha Lipi — Historical Research Methodology

## 1. Epigraphical Foundation & Purpose

Nava Graha Lipi is an independent initiative to develop a historically informed Indian astronomical symbol system, rooted in the epigraphic traditions of the Tamil region.

Our primary historical reference is **Pallava Grantha** (4th–9th century CE), supported by parallel comparative research in:
- **Vatteluttu** (5th–13th century CE)
- **Grantha** (6th century CE–present)
- **Tamil-Brahmi** (3rd century BCE–3rd century CE)

## 2. Research Integrity Principles

The credibility of this project rests upon strict historical integrity:

1. **No Historical Invention**: Newly designed astronomical symbols are explicitly identified as modern original designs. They must never be presented as ancient artifacts or rediscovered historical symbols.
2. **Strict Provenance**: Every historical character in the Script Atlas must cite a verifiable primary source (e.g., Archaeological Survey of India publications, *Epigraphia Indica*, or published academic proposals such as Anshuman Pandey's Unicode proposal L2/18-083).
3. **Palaeographic Segregation**: Pallava Grantha must not be conflated with later medieval or modern Grantha. Although Grantha evolved from Pallava, 5th-century Pallava inscriptions exhibit distinct structural ductus, loop formations, and terminal finishes that must be preserved.
4. **Cultural & Linguistic Specificity**: Do not substitute Devanagari or Hindi conventions for Tamil-region traditions. Sanskrit verses should be supported through Grantha script representations, and Tamil astronomical terminology (ஞாயிறு, திங்கள், செவ்வாய், புதன், வியாழன், வெள்ளி, சனி, இராகு, கேது) must be preserved alongside Sanskrit.

## 3. Character Attestation Categories

Every character record in the database is classified into one of five attestation statuses:

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| `directly-attested` | Directly observed in verified inscriptions, copper plates, or estampages published by recognized epigraphical bodies. |
| `reconstructed` | Philologically and palaeographically deduced from systematic Brahmi derivation rules, but awaiting direct physical inscriptional discovery. |
| `later-form` | Attested in later development phases (e.g., transitional Chola Grantha or modern liturgical Grantha), but distinguished from archaic Pallava forms. |
| `modern-addition` | Modern typographic or liturgical innovation added to facilitate digital interchange. |
| `uncertain` | Inscriptional instance is damaged, ambiguous, or subject to scholarly dispute. |

## 4. Confidence Grading

| Grade | Criteria |
|---|---|
| `high` | Multi-source attestation in published epigraphs (e.g., *Epigraphia Indica*, Sivaramamurti 1952, or Pandey 2018). |
| `medium` | Single known inscriptional occurrence or partially abraded plate reading. |
| `low` | Attestation based on secondary sketches without published photographic or estampage plates. |
| `uncertain` | Conflicting scholarly interpretations or disputed decipherment. |

## 5. Primary Reference Corpus

1. **Pandey, Anshuman** (2018): *Preliminary Proposal to Encode Pallava in Unicode* (L2/18-083).
2. **Archaeological Survey of India**: *Epigraphia Indica* (Vols. I–XLIII) and *South Indian Inscriptions*.
3. **Bühler, Georg** (1896): *Indische Palaeographie*.
4. **Burnell, Arthur Coke** (1878): *Elements of South Indian Palaeography*.
5. **Sivaramamurti, Calambur** (1952): *Indian Epigraphy and South Indian Scripts*.
6. **Mahadevan, Iravatham** (2003): *Early Tamil Epigraphy*.
